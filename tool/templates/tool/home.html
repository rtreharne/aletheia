Why Aletheia

Submitted work no longer proves that a student understands it.

Generative AI can produce fluent essays, reports and code in seconds. AI detectors cannot reliably tell who wrote what, and a false accusation harms the student and the institution.

The dependable test has always been to ask: can you explain what you wrote? An oral examination does this well, but it costs hours of staff time for every cohort.

Aletheia asks that question of every student, in about ten minutes, as part of handing in. It does not guess who wrote the work. It gives each student the chance to show they understand it, and gives you evidence to weigh alongside your own judgement.

How it works

  1. Hand in

    The student uploads their work in your course, checks the preview and agrees to your AI policy. Where your LMS supports it, the file also goes into your gradebook as a normal submission.

  2. Objectives

    Aletheia reads the submission and your brief, and writes learning objectives for that specific piece of work.

  3. Questions

    The student answers short, timed questions on each objective. A wrong answer brings a second question on the same objective. There is no going back.

  4. Alignment

    The student sees a score for each objective, with explanations. You see every answer, how long each took and whenever they left the page.

Step 3, as the student sees it

One question at a time

The line along the top is the time left. When it runs out, whatever is selected is submitted, and there is no going back. You see how long each answer took. The student doesn't.

OBJECTIVE 1 / 5

You argue the Irish famine was the occasion, not the cause, of repeal. What evidence do you use to support this distinction?

Select one.

  1. Peel insisted on permanent repeal when temporary suspension would have met the crisis
  2. Peel's Cabinet unanimously backed repeal once news of the blight arrived
  3. Repeal lowered grain prices enough to end starvation in Ireland within two years
  4. The famine struck before the League had won a majority in the Commons

Optional Before and during step 3

In the room

Students can hand in whenever they like, but their questions open only in a scheduled, invigilated session, with a word shown on the screen in the room and nowhere else.

  1. On the screen

    Open the room word from your dashboard and put it on the projector.

  2. To begin

    Students type the word they can see to open their questions. Without it, they can't start.

  3. Along the way

    Once or twice, between objectives, they're asked again and have 10 seconds to type it. A miss is flagged for you, and the quiz carries on.

What you control

Brief
What the assignment asked for, and what the objectives should focus on.
Objectives
Between 1 and 10 per submission.
Questions
How many correct answers clear an objective, and how many retries follow a wrong one.
Time
A countdown for each question, or one limit for the whole check. Answer times are shown to you, never to students.
Code
Accept source files, notebooks and .zip projects, with questions that quote the student's own code.
Attempts
One, several or unlimited.
AI policy
Your generative AI policy, which students must agree to before handing in.

Your grading

Your marking stays exactly as it is.

  • Every upload is kept with the student's answers, and where your LMS supports it, handed in to your gradebook as a normal submission.
  • Aletheia never sets, changes or clears a grade.
  • Alignment, answer times and the integrity log stay in Aletheia, for you to weigh.
  • The class list comes from your course, so you can see who has not yet handed in.
